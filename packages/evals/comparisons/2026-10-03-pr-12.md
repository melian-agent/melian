## Differences

Codex found the trust-model gap and the submodule and mode gaps, all at the boundary between untrusted content and the review. Its two high findings changed the design: policy and standards now come from a git revision the host chooses, the base for a pull request. The automated Claude review found determinism and robustness defects inside the parser and the merge: unpinned diff settings, a walk that never ends, a schema that hid its own test, and prototype pollution. The sets were disjoint.

The Opus review overlapped both, on three findings, and widened Codex's boundary: a hook's environment, the head's own `.gitattributes`, and non-UTF-8 paths are all ways the repository under review reaches into how Melian reads it. It was also the only reviewer to read the design against the code, finding the `.melian/` contradiction and the API that steps 4 and 5 will need.

Each valid finding marked as golden is to be written for the evals corpus once step 5 of the [implementation plan](../../../docs/design-implementation-plan.md) defines the format.
