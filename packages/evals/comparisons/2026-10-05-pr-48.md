# Comparison review: pull request #48

Pull request: [melian-agent/melian#48](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/48), which closes the gap [#42](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/42) left in the rule that no `melian.yaml` switches off the review of itself. A nested `melian.yaml` is judged under the configuration of the directory above it. The root has no directory above it, so it was judged under its own settings, and a root that switched `policy-change-review` off hid every edit to itself. The pull request first judged the root under the built-in defaults. After review, it judges the root under its own configuration with the defaults as a floor. A `melian.yaml`'s policy notice now resolves under the configuration that judged it, and never below `acknowledge`.

Reviewers: Codex adversarial review; Melian's own review, with all six built-in lenses at `careful`; and an Opus review of record. All three reviewed the branch at f6ff09f. The fixes landed in four commits: 9c5976d, 80ca7c2, 74097c2, and 9ab640b. Melian reviewed 9ab640b in a second round, and the fixes for its four findings are in progress. Neither Codex nor the Opus review ran on a later head.

Adjudication gives the severity and the maintainer's disposition. Every finding was accepted but Codex's, which the maintainer judged by design for the code it named; the documents were reworded to claim only what holds. None was judged noise. The Golden column says whether a golden should be written. Two are owed, both for the `correctness` lens: a root whose resolution silences the notice on its own change (C1), and a policy notice merged with another check's finding at one site (D1). Tests or documents cover the rest.

This record links [the decision](../../../docs/decisions/2026-10-05-root-policy-file-review-floor.md) that arrives with #48, so the link resolves once both land.

## Maintainer decisions

- **The root `melian.yaml` may tighten the review of itself, never loosen it.** The root is judged under its own configuration, `melian.local.yaml` included, with the built-in defaults as a floor. The guardrail stays on, and each severity is the stricter of the root's and the default's. From B1, C2, and C3.
- **A `melian.yaml`'s policy notice resolves under the configuration that judged it.** It never resolves below `acknowledge`. From C1.
- **The rule holds under the policy source the host chose.** A pull-request review reads the base, so a head cannot hide a change to its own root policy. A checked-out range reads the working tree by design, because its author is the maintainer. From A1.

## Differences

Two reviewers found the resolution gap on their own. The guardrail judged the root under one configuration, and adjudication resolved its notice under another. The Opus review reproduced it at f6ff09f: a root that maps `P2` to `silent` passed an edit to itself (C1). Melian's first round missed it. After 80ca7c2 fixed it, Melian's second round found the variant the fix left open: a notice merged with another check's finding takes the keeper's rule and loses its floor (D1).

Codex named the silent mapping too, as one of two ways a working-tree head could hide its notice (A1). It put the cause in the policy source, which the maintainer kept by design, and only the Opus review's reproduction showed the gap held for a trusted base.

Melian and the Opus review also found the same loosening apart: judging the root under the defaults dropped a stricter severity the base asked for (B1, C2). Melian's `trust-boundary` lens filed it as `fail-open` and cited the test that asserted the drop. It drove the pull request's main design change, from the defaults to a floor.

Round two's `conventions` lens quoted the repository's own writing and code rules against the change. It held the decision file to `AGENTS.md`'s 25-word sentence rule (D2), and a one-call, one-line helper to its rule on inlining (D3). This is the first time a Melian lens enforced `AGENTS.md`'s prose rules on a pull request. On #42 the lens checked a guideline against the code and found documents left stale; here it applied the rules written for every author.

Against the six distinct findings of the first round, Melian's recall is one in six and its precision one in one. It missed C1, the one high finding, and reached its variant only after the fix. Its other misses were the policy source (A1), the local layer (C3), and two findings in the tests (C4, C5).

One finding overlaps another: C2 is B1. A1's silent half shares C1's fix. No reviewer raised a finding the maintainer judged noise.
