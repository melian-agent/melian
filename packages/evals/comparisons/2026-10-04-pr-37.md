## Maintainer decisions

Four decisions came from the first round. Each is recorded in [the lens budgets decision](../../../docs/decisions/2026-10-04-lens-budgets.md), `design.md`, and the pipeline guideline.

- **A budget end is an incomplete check.** A lens its budget ended records `ended`, and the verdict is not reviewed: a required status must never turn green from reduced coverage. A level may opt in with `budget.ended: count`, and its lens records `ran` with the ending; the built-in levels do not. A budget that ends a lens at `quick` before it reports anything is an escalation trigger for triage to add in step 5. From A4 and C5.
- **Termination covers every result path.** Once a round is spent, the run ends whatever the round's other calls did, through Pi's handoff control, on a read, a refusal, an error, and `report_finding`'s own throws. Pi offers no boundary for a call that fails validation or names an unoffered tool, so that residual is documented and pinned by a test. From A2, B1, and C1.
- **`careful` has real limits.** 200k tokens and 30 tool calls on both built-in lenses, and `quick` at 100k tokens. From A1.
- **`report_finding` counts toward tools.** Each call counts one, a report is never refused, and corrections of one finding stop at three. From A3.

## Differences

Codex found four, each a way the budgets bounded less than they claimed: a level with no limits, a round that could not end, reads hidden inside reports, and an exhausted lens that passed. Its fourth changed the design. A budget end had been an observation beside `ran`; it became an incomplete check that leaves the verdict not reviewed, with an opt-in per level. Its first three changed the built-in budgets, how a spent round ends, and what counts as a tool call.

The Opus review of record found ten, eight of its own. Its findings test the mechanism's arithmetic: what a call is keyed by, which reads the model sees, what a record's counts claim, and how levels layer through `extends`. Its fifth is Codex's fourth, reached from the other end: a lens ended with no findings passed, and the pull request body said nothing. Those two drove the maintainer decision that changed the design. Its first probed the termination path Melian found and showed four requests where one was due.

In the first round Melian found one, a real defect in the new code that the Opus review also raised. Melian rated it P2; the adjudication rates it high, with Codex's second finding, because both left a spent lens unbounded. Against the thirteen distinct findings of the first round, Melian's recall is one in thirteen and its precision one in one. Its two lenses, `correctness` and `contracts`, review code for defects and contract breaks, not configuration, documents, or conventions, where five of the thirteen sit.

Melian's rounds two and three each found a defect that the previous fix pass introduced, in code no other reviewer saw. In round two, A4's fix made a budget end an incomplete check, and C3's fix let the crossing round go on with a note telling the lens to report and finish; a lens that obeyed never sent the read that would record the end, so it recorded a full run. In round three, D1's fix recorded an end on every refused read, while C2's fix numbered reads by `LiveDoc` slot, so a blocked call shrank the budget and now recorded a false end. The loop closed on the fourth round with a clean verdict.

Two findings overlap: B1 and C1, and A4 and C5. A2 and B1 share a fix but name different paths: Codex's calls that never reach a tool, Melian's calls that reach it and throw. No reviewer raised a finding the maintainer judged noise.
