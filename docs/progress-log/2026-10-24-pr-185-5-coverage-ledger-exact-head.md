# 2026-10-24: the coverage ledger takes mutation evidence at the exact head and lets no notice cover a hunk

Codex's adversarial review of [pull request #185](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/185) at `577a0750` found two holes. First, the ledger accepted a verification record from an earlier head when the mutant's line mapped unchanged, yet a change to `b.ts` can make an earlier-killed mutant in unchanged `a.ts` survive. [The decision](../decisions/2026-10-23-coverage-ledger-and-delta-review.md) now adopts the contract of [pull request #189](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/189): "A verification record is one immutable batch, a test's three control windows and both mutated results for every candidate in one round, and it is evidence only at the head it names, only whole under its batch ID, and only when its executor (the local ID Melian stores beside the install that ran it), its installation identity (the lockfiles, the test runner's version, the Node version, the platform and the architecture), its adapter identity, its policy digest (the SHA-256 of the effective base-sourced mutation policy, declared environment variables included, and the resolved test-command argv) and its test's full component array each equal the reader's; a record that differs in any is rerun, never reused, and no mutant's result is reused outside its batch." Mutation coverage never maps or carries. Settled survivor findings, dismissals, `invalid` notes and config-ignored mutants cover as #189 says, and an `invalid-claim` or `flaky` record covers nothing. Voids 7 and 8 shrink for mutation evidence to "a record from another head is never evidence", and a new void 11 covers a base whose mutation policy changes. The `Amends:` line now names #189's decision instead of the rerun decision. Second, a policy-change notice no longer covers an oversized analyser-configuration or lockfile hunk. Such a hunk reaches each lens in bounded chunks through `read_file`, and stays a gap until that lens's reads deliver every line; the one escape is a maintainer's dismissal naming the check and the lines, recorded as a dismissal. Attack rules 15 and 16 state that `review.coverage` cannot pass on a stale kill or on an oversized hunk no lens received, and the plan's tests say the same.
