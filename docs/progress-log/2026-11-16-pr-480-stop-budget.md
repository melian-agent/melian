On [issue #480](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/480)'s branch, [pull request #498](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/498), a stop of core's `git` now has one budget of three seconds. Codex's review of `c9aa445b` found that `terminateRecorded` reads the process table again before every signal and while it waits, so a slow `ps` that answers inside its five-second limit could stretch one stop to minutes, while the call waited for it.

It was the second review in a row to find a stop whose time nothing bounded; the one before found the unbounded read of a `ps` that stalls. The builder raised the class with the coordinator, which chose the budget on 2026-11-16 as a choice of approach. `git` wraps the table it hands `stopTree` so that every read more than three seconds, on a monotonic clock, after the stop began throws, and a throwing read already ends the stop with `childrenLeft`. A call now settles at most the budget plus one read limit, about eight seconds, after its deadline or output bound. `terminateRecorded` and the rest of `process-tree.ts` are unchanged: `git diff c9aa445b -- packages/core/src/process-tree.ts packages/pipeline` prints nothing.

- The tests came first. On `c9aa445b`, "reads and signals nothing more one millisecond past the stop's budget, and says the children may still run" drove a fake table on a fake clock that jumps to 3,001 ms after the third read. The stop ran its whole course and the result had no `childrenLeft`. With the budget the fourth read throws, only the first SIGTERM was sent, and the result carries `childrenLeft`. Its twin at 3,000 ms runs the whole stop, and "runs a stop whose reads all fall inside its budget as before" holds the stop's four signals on the real clock. "gives up on the real clock once the stop's reads have taken three seconds" makes two reads take 1.6 seconds each.
- The warning now reads "Melian could not find or stop every program git started, so some may still run", since it covers a spent budget and a git that had already exited, as well as an unreadable table.
- Stryker's instrumenter gave 15 mutants on the commit's lines. One, deleting `child.kill()`, sits on a line the commit did not change, and an earlier pass proved it. At `e65c615f` the other 13 that change behaviour were killed by their named tests, each run alone after it passed unmutated, on a copy of `process-tree.ts` whose `ProcessTable.kill` sent no signal. A default clock that returned nothing survived until the real-clock test came in `e65c615f`. Emptying the budget's error text is equivalent: the error is caught where the stop settles and becomes `childrenLeft`, so its text is never read. [The hand inventory](2026-11-16-pr-480-hand-mutants.json) holds all 14.
