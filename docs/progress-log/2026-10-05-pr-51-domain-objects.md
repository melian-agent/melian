Core's domain types carry their behaviour, in [pull request #51](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/51), milestone 2 step 12. `Finding`, `Defect`, `Verdict`, `Manifest`, `Lens`, `Revision`, and `Changeset` are classes over their stored JSON, and the free functions over them are methods: `Finding.create`, `Finding.parse`, and `Finding.from` build a finding, which resolves, ranks, places, and merges claims itself; an `Adjudication` decides a `Verdict`, which renders, plans its publication, gives its commit status, fingerprints itself, and finds the `Defect` a dismissal names; `Lens.load` and `Lens.select` load and pick lenses, which render their own instructions; and `Changeset.resolve` builds a changeset whose `Revision` classifies a finding's cause. The outer packages adapt core's objects through their own classes, `ReviewComment` in github, `ChangePrompt` in the pipeline, and `ReviewOutcome` and `StoredReview` in the CLI. No stored shape changed: each class declares its fields in its stored order and writes them back in that order, so every document, task input, lens version, and verdict fingerprint is the same JSON, which the publication tests that read an older Melian's documents prove, and the goldens' renderings are byte-identical. The `free-domain-function` Biome rule's exclusion list is empty, and the `conventions-free-domain-function` golden expects `conventions` to quote a repository's own wording of the rule against a free function over a `Finding`. [The decision on domain objects over their JSON](../decisions/2026-10-05-domain-objects-over-json.md) records how such a class is built and keeps its hashes.
