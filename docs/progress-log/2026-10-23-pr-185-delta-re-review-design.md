Every fix pass on pull requests [#162](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/162) to [#174](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/174) had been followed by a full round of 15 to 70 minutes, five of about 35 minutes on [pull request #174](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/174) alone. `melian review --delta` builds on an anchor round Melian picks from its store under eight rules, each falling back to a full round. It carries untouched findings to the new head by git's line map and re-judges changed ones through escalation's hand-off. Static checks run over the whole range, and Stryker mutates the delta's lines alone. A delta round's key, `<base>..<head>@<anchor round>`, keeps its tasks, sightings and verdict apart from the full round at the same base and head. Its verdict carries a provisional `review.delta` record, so it is not reviewed and publishes `error`; only a full round approves. The decision amends review identity, review attachment, the rounds decision and the mutation rerun decision, each with a scope. The plan splits the build into three stacked pull requests of about 1,500, 2,200 and 900 lines. The decision lists nine trust and blocking rules for the attack before any code.

Second, a merged verdict cannot resolve each carried report on its own, so the carry now copies the anchor's sightings per producer while the anchor is still current.
