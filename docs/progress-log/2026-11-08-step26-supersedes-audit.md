# Step 26: the audit of partial `Supersedes:` lines

[Issue #238](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/238) closes step 26. The attack on [pull request #150](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/150) counted 36 `Supersedes:` lines that retired a whole decision while changing part of it, but recorded no list. This audit read all 83 edges that resolve on main at `27797250`, 80 of which existed at the attack. Each pair was read whole, with the active decisions its chain ends in, and each lost clause was checked against code and docs. 24 edges are whole retirements. 59 are narrow, all from before the attack: the successor changed part of its target and restated none of the rest, so 385 distinct clauses lost their standing. More edges qualify than the attack's 36 because a successor that says the rest "stands" or "remains" without restating it counts as narrow. The [phase 1 census](2026-10-18-step26-phase1-supersession-rule.md#hand-census) judged 56 of its rows useful by the same test.

A superseded file stays inactive whatever later happens to its successor, and merged decision files are never edited. So a narrow edge is repaired by a new restore decision that names the active heads its chain ends in and restates the lost clauses that still hold. 17 restore decisions, one per family of related targets, restate 286 clauses, merged into 275 bullets where two sources said the same thing. 99 were dropped: obsolete on main, already stated by another active decision, in conflict with one, or unsettled. The files began as amendments, and an amendment wins where it conflicts with its target, so a doubtful clause was dropped rather than restated. The mutation decision needs no restore decision: [the reruns decision](../decisions/2026-10-16-mutation-reruns-later-changes.md) restates every clause of it that holds. No existing decision file changed, and the head graph resolves with an index of 42,875 bytes against its 64 KiB bound.

The first table below gives every edge's verdict and reason. The second lists every dropped clause with why it went, the conflicting and unsettled ones first with the maintainer's ruling. The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-08, in the builder's session, on the eleven drops marked conflicting or unsettled. The publishing-credentials clause is restated in the ledger restore decision. The ledger's section order goes to a follow-up that records the order the renderer uses. The other nine stay dropped. Codex's third review and Melian's first round each found a restated clause false on main. So, by the maintainer's choice, fresh agents checked every bullet of the 17 amendments against code, the active decisions and the design. 260 of 275 held. The other 15 were rewritten: seven wrote a planned or deferred behaviour down as current, four conflicted with an active rule, one was false, and three carried a stale detail. Melian's second round still found three restated clauses that would override a newer active decision: the trust floor's protected minimum, twice, and how recall counts. So the maintainer chose, on 2026-11-08 in the builder's session, to make each amendment yield. Every one of the 17 now says that any other active decision, the ones it amends included, wins where they conflict, and the three clauses are qualified. Round 3 then found that the yield line contradicted the `Amends:` rule, under which an amendment wins, and Codex found that one amendment naming several heads tied them together: superseding one head would refuse the graph or drop the others' clauses. So the maintainer chose, on 2026-11-08 in the builder's session, to make the 17 files plain decisions. Each names its heads in prose, declares no graph edge, and yields to every other active decision. Two gaps surfaced outside this step. `docs/design/interaction-model.md` lists the ledger's sections in an order the renderer no longer follows. The comment on the check workflow's `origin/main` fetch names a triage gate that no longer needs it.
