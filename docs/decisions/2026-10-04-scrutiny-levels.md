# Scrutiny levels

Choice: A lens declares levels in `LENS.md`, `quick`, `careful`, and `deep`, each setting the model tier, token and tool budgets, the finding cap, whether it reads enclosing functions or hunks only, and whether its candidates are verified. Triage at intake answers one choice question per lens, `skip`, `quick`, `careful`, or `deep`, through the `Decider` port: a decision model when one is configured, else the LLM fallback adapter, else the tier's default level. Policy sets a floor and a ceiling per path, and triage moves only within that band, so triage can never switch off a lens policy says runs. One mechanical escalation: a lens at `quick` that reports at or above a configured severity reruns at the next level as a new check record. The manifest records the level each check ran at. The fast tier skips triage.

Why: Conditional scrutiny is the cheapest form of conditional review, and the split keeps the verdict deterministic: a model proposes, policy bounds, the resolver executes. Claude Code's effort levels and a private repository's quick, standard, and deep modes show the shape ([research/2026-10-04-review-tools-compared.md](../research/2026-10-04-review-tools-compared.md)). The level names differ from the model tier `light` and the check tier `standard`, so a reader never mistakes one for the other.
