# Triage as built

Choice: A lens's key is `name@version@level`. Its findings' `source.version` is `<version>@<level>`. So the review index, the lens task's children, attempts, and results, request IDs, and sightings all tell levels apart. The review index also keys a selection by each lens's band and `triage.escalateAt`. A record an older Melian stored keeps its keys and stays readable; no review after the upgrade attaches to it. A lens task an older Melian left running resumes with its runs' levels stripped, so its findings keep the bare version.

Triage is one `melian.decision` task per revision and question set. A repeat review attaches to it. Only `rerun` asks again, and only after a decision that did not complete. A decider that fails, times out, or answers what it was not asked leaves every lens at its default level within its band, never at `skip`. Each lens's record says so.

Triage offers only the levels inside a lens's band whose tier routes to a model with credentials. The band is read from the policy source for every file the lens reviews. A lens with no such level fails the review before any lens runs, and so does a policy that cannot be read. A review of a head the host does not trust has a default floor of `careful` until a calibrated decision model answers. That is a pull request, or a range whose policy is read from a revision. A range review of the checked-out commit keeps `quick`. A review in which every lens stayed at `quick` says so on each record.

Escalation runs inside the lens task, in a new conversation the commit that records why creates. The run it escalated to stands for the lens: its record, with notes, and its findings. That run is given the quick run's findings at or above `escalateAt` to confirm or refute. A finding it restates counts once, from it. One it refutes is dropped. One it does neither still counts, attributed to the quick run, until step 6's verifier judges it. An escalation whose next level's tier has no model is capped with a note.

Without a review plan, the CLI's LLM fallback runs on the cheapest lens tier routed to a credentialed model. The review records every `decisions.*` check itself. With `decisions.provider` set, that record is a skip the review does not allow. The CLI refuses the provider before anything runs, until milestone 4 brings an adapter.

Why: A key without the level let a careful review attach to a quick one. It also let an escalated rerun overwrite the quick run's sightings, or be deduplicated as the same request. A decision asked afresh on every call would change the selection and rerun every lens after a crash. Failing closed to `skip` would turn a provider outage into lost coverage. A band read from unreadable policy cannot be trusted. Guessing the default, or a level whose tier has no model, would run a lens below a floor policy set. The head of a pull request writes the change triage reads, so an uncalibrated model it can steer must not send every lens to a quick look. Reading the escalated run's findings alone let a quick blocker vanish when the higher run merely left it out. Escalation could then turn a blocking review into a pass, so only an explicit refutation removes one. A configured provider with no adapter left `decisions.fast` with no record ([issue #24](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/24)). Refusing it says what to do, and the recorded skip keeps a library caller from reading "no record".

Supersedes: 2026-10-04-scrutiny-levels.md, in its sentence that an escalated lens "reruns at the next level as a new check record". The higher run's record stands for the lens in place of the quick run's, and the quick run's severe findings count until the higher run refutes them.
