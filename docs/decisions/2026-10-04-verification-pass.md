# Verification pass

Choice: Every candidate finding a lens reports at a level that verifies passes a verifier before adjudication counts it. Verification is one typed state and one typed verdict with two executors. The state is the candidate, its failure scenario, its evidence locations, the enclosing functions at those locations, and the callers Enola supplies when present. The questions have the `Decider` shape: does the code at the location do what the claim says; is there a guard that prevents the failure; is the failure pre-existing; and a choice over `confirmed`, `plausible`, and `refuted`, with a reason and an optional correction. An LLM verifier conversation with the read-only tools answers through a `report_verdict` tool; a decision model answers when the packed state fits its capability descriptor and a provider is present, from milestone 4. The verdict is stored on the finding as `verification`: verdict, reason, correction, executor, model, and version. `confidence` stays reserved for a calibrated decision-model probability, and the LLM verifier never writes it. The verifier runs on its own tier, `verifier`, which the resolver routes to a different model family from the finder whenever one is available. Thresholds are asymmetric at first: a decision model may confirm or escalate to the LLM verifier, but a refutation needs the LLM verifier until calibration data shows the decision model's refutations hold. A lens finding no verifier judged is capped at advisory, as a `pre-existing` finding is, so unverified findings cannot block; the `quick` level does not verify by default, and escalation reruns a severe quick finding at a level that does. A `refuted` finding is dropped from the verdict and kept in the store with its verdict; `plausible` and `confirmed` findings count, and the ledger shows which. `report_finding` gains two required fields, a failure scenario and evidence locations as `{ file, line, endLine }`, because a verifier has nothing to attack without them.

Why: Claude Code's review skill and a private repository's review skill both attack each candidate before reporting it, and their precision depends on it ([research/2026-10-04-review-tools-compared.md](../research/2026-10-04-review-tools-compared.md)); Melian has no verifier, and its `confidence` field is never filled. Uniform records across executors become the calibration set. Dropping a real P1 on a 9B-parameter model's refutation is the failure to design against. As first stated, this decision said every candidate passes a verifier before it counts, while [the scrutiny levels decision](2026-10-04-scrutiny-levels.md) let a level skip verification. The advisory cap resolves the two: an unverified finding still counts, but only a verified one can block.
