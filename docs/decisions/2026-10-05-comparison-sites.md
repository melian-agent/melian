# Where a comparison matches, and the head it imports at

Choice: An external finding matches a Melian finding by site at the Melian finding's own location or at any of its `cause` evidence locations at head, within three lines. External findings that match no Melian finding group by site only across reviewers, a reviewer being its name and login. One reviewer's two findings at one site stay two. `melian compare` refuses a pull request whose head on GitHub differs from the head of Melian's stored review, and imports nothing. On GitHub, CodeRabbit's bot is `coderabbit` and Copilot's is `copilot`; any other author is `human`, kept by its login and whether it is a bot. An external finding's ID hashes its source reference alone, never the reviewer. A review thread matches by site only when its first comment was written on the compared head; a thread from an earlier head is listed as external-only, with that commit, until the maintainer matches it by hand. A matched count is of distinct external findings, with the Melian findings they cover reported beside it, and an external finding that matches two Melian findings by site is marked ambiguous until the maintainer matches or unmatches it by hand.

Why: The comparison decision matched within three lines of a Melian finding's lines and left three cases open. Problem: an `affected` finding sits in a file the change did not edit, so a reviewer of the diff points at the changed line that breaks it. Example: Melian reports a broken caller in `src/server.ts` with its `cause` at the renamed export in `src/config.ts`, and CodeRabbit comments on `src/config.ts`; matched by location alone, both read as misses. Solution: the `cause` locations at head are sites too, while a `context` location or one at the base is not, since neither is where a reviewer of the diff would point. Grouping follows the merge's rule that findings from one check never merge: one reviewer's two comments on adjacent lines are usually two defects, and counting them once would hide a miss. GitHub places a thread's lines at the pull request's current head, so a pull request that moved since Melian's review would match its threads against lines of another commit; refusing says to review the head first. Copilot's reviewer bot is common enough to name now; any other bot is rare enough that its login and kind, kept beside the name, tell it apart until statistics need more. A name is a taxonomy that may change, and an ID that hashed it would orphan every hand match recorded under the old name, so the source reference alone identifies a finding: GitHub's node ID for a thread, or the file and the finding's ref or content. A thread stays on the pull request after a push, and GitHub carries its line forward even when the push fixed what it named. Example: CodeRabbit flags a null dereference at the first head, the author fixes it, and the thread, resolved, now sits on the fixed line; matched by site against Melian's review of the new head, it would credit CodeRabbit with a defect that head no longer has, or pair it with a different defect nearby. Only the maintainer can say whether the earlier finding still applies. An external finding within three lines of two Melian findings sits beside each, and counting groups gave one comment the weight of two matches. Proximity cannot say which defect it names, so the count follows distinct external findings and the maintainer decides the pairing.
