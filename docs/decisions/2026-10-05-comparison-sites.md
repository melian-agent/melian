# Where a comparison matches, and the head it imports at

Choice: An external finding matches a Melian finding by site at the Melian finding's own location or at any of its `cause` evidence locations at head, within three lines. External findings that match no Melian finding group by site only across reviewers, a reviewer being its name and login. One reviewer's two findings at one site stay two. `melian compare` refuses a pull request whose head on GitHub differs from the head of Melian's stored review, and imports nothing. A GitHub login other than CodeRabbit's names a `human` reviewer, kept by its login.

Why: The comparison decision matched within three lines of a Melian finding's lines and left three cases open. Problem: an `affected` finding sits in a file the change did not edit, so a reviewer of the diff points at the changed line that breaks it. Example: Melian reports a broken caller in `src/server.ts` with its `cause` at the renamed export in `src/config.ts`, and CodeRabbit comments on `src/config.ts`; matched by location alone, both read as misses. Solution: the `cause` locations at head are sites too, while a `context` location or one at the base is not, since neither is where a reviewer of the diff would point. Grouping follows the merge's rule that findings from one check never merge: one reviewer's two comments on adjacent lines are usually two defects, and counting them once would hide a miss. GitHub places a thread's lines at the pull request's current head, so a pull request that moved since Melian's review would match its threads against lines of another commit; refusing says to review the head first. The design names four reviewers, and a bot other than CodeRabbit is rare enough that its login, kept beside the name, tells it apart until statistics need more.
