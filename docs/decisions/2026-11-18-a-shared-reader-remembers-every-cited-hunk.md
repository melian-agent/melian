# A shared reader remembers the hunks cited since a dismissal

Amends: [the dismissal record decision](2026-11-16-a-dismissal-record-holds-what-melian-dismiss-stored.md), "A dismissal record is never withdrawn" and the rule `RecordedRound` applies a record by

Status: for [issue #481](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/481). The coordinator answered on 2026-11-18, choosing this design over exporting the host's memory into a record, which the maintainer's ruling of 2026-11-18 keeps off the export seam; that is a coordinator's answer, not a ruling. The coordinator corrected the rule the same day, after Codex's second review, so that the memory belongs to a standing dismissal and starts at it. It is the rule the coordinator has put on [issue #395](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/395) for the gate.

## Problem

The host remembers every proving hunk a sighting of a dismissed finding cites, and reopens the dismissal when any of them changes. The reader met a dismissal record against the record's own proof and the current sighting's alone, so it forgot the rest.

Example: a finding is dismissed citing hunk A. The next revision's sighting cites A and B, both unchanged, so it stays dismissed, and the host now remembers B. On the third revision B changes and the sighting cites only A. The host reopens the finding and blocks; the reader kept the dismissal and passed. Codex's review of [pull request #575](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/575) found it.

## Decision

The reader works the memory out from the records it already lists, and the memory belongs to a standing dismissal, not to its finding. A dismissal holds only while every proving hunk cited by a sighting of its finding, in a lens or check record a counting round names, is unchanged at the revision being read. Only rounds since the dismissal count. The reader takes them from the round chain: from the rounds the round it reads names in `before`, back along each round's `before`, stopping at the rounds of the revision the dismissal's sighting names, which it leaves out. A dismissal record's own sighting adds nothing, since its trigger's proof already holds every hunk that sighting cites. No record changes shape.

The round it reads, and the sighting being tested, add nothing either. The host tests a sighting against the accepted proof and what it remembered before that sighting. A hunk the tested round cites is always one its revision holds, so counting it only turns the rule from comparing triggers to checking hunks, which never reopens. Example: a finding is dismissed as introduced, so its accepted proof is empty. The next round sights it as affected, citing hunk A, with a new trigger. The host compares triggers and reopens; a reader that counted A checked A, found it, and kept the dismissal. The review of record on [pull request #575](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/575) found it with a probe.

The host clears its memory when a finding reopens, and a dismissal made after that starts from what the host has since seen. A memory kept for the finding, for all time, would make such a dismissal reopen at every revision after, so the gate could never pass it again. Codex's second review of [pull request #575](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/575) found that, in the narrower form of a lens record exported before its review died, which no round names.

`citedProof` in `packages/pipeline/src/findings.ts` gathers the hunks, and `sharedDismissalHolds` applies the host's reopen rule with them. `RecordedRound` hands each dismissal its `cited` hunks, in its verdict and in `contents`, so a gate that decides sightings itself calls the same function and agrees by construction.

## A broken chain

When the chain names a round the set does not hold, or a round of another changeset, or a round names a record the set does not hold, the reader cannot know what that round cited. The dismissal then counts for nothing, and its finding stays live. That is the strict direction: it blocks more, never less. It is never a failure of the whole set: the round still reads, and only the dismissals that chain would carry drop.

The same holds when any branch of the chain ends, at a round with an empty `before`, before it reaches a round of the dismissal's revision. An earlier form of this rule took such a chain whole, and Codex's fourth review of [pull request #575](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/575) showed why that was loose: a chain that ends early may have been cut, and the rounds past the cut may have cited a hunk that has since changed. The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-18, in the coordinator's session, that the reader counts such a dismissal as nothing.

## A host with no history of the changeset, and a host that missed rounds

A round named in `before` only the rounds of the revision its own host reviewed before. A host with no local history of the pull request, such as a fresh clone or an Actions round's throwaway store, therefore wrote `before: []`, though the store already held earlier rounds. Example: a dismissal is made at revision 1. A second host's round at revision 2 cites hunks A and B. A third, fresh host reviews revision 3, which changed B, and cites only A. Its round named no predecessor, so the reader never saw the second round, and kept the dismissal while the host reopened it.

By the same ruling, a round names the store's frontier in `before`: every round of another revision in the review's one store listing, the listing that also fills each dismissal's `supersedes`, that no other such round names. It joins that frontier with the rounds of the revision its own host reviewed before, so it does so whether or not the host has history of the changeset. A first form named the frontier only from a host with no history, and that left a gap. Example: host A reviews revision 1. A fresh host B reviews revision 2 and cites hunk B. Host A reviews revision 3, which changed B. A's round named only its own revision-1 round, so the reader never saw B's citation. The coordinator agreed on 2026-11-18 that a round names the frontier always; that is a coordinator's answer under the maintainer's ruling that the reader is at least as strict as the host, not a new ruling. The rounds come from that one listing, never a second. A store that cannot be listed fails the review before any round is written, so no round names a frontier it did not read; a round some other way left with `before: []` falls to the rule above. A forked history gives several predecessors, and each branch only adds hunks for the reader to remember, so it can only block more. A later pull request lets the `melian round` workflow hand a round the IDs of the store's newest rounds instead, by the maintainer's ruling of 2026-11-18 on [issue #397](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/397); `predecessors` in `packages/pipeline/src/review.ts` is the one place either source feeds.

A round's ID hashes its whole envelope, `before` included, so a fresh host's round now has a different ID than it had. Its key does not change: it holds the revision and the IDs the round read, as [the identity guideline](../guidelines/identity.md#the-keys-melian-has) says.

## The limit

A round that was never shared cannot be remembered, and neither can a record no round names. If the second revision's round in the example reached no shared store, the reader holds only A and keeps the dismissal where the host reopens it. With every round shared, the reader is at least as strict as the host.

It can be stricter. The host drops a hunk that changed while the finding stood undismissed; the reader remembers every hunk a round since the dismissal cited. That blocks more, never less.

## Tests

`findings.test.ts` reads a dismissal of an introduced finding sighted later as affected with a new trigger: the host and the reader both reopen it. It also drives the host through the three revisions and reads the third with the hunks of the sightings shared: the host and the reader both read `new`, and without the second revision's sighting the reader reads `dismissed`. `recorded-round.test.ts` pins the rule through records and rounds, and remembers no hunk the round it reads cites. A lens record, and a check record, that a round after the dismissal names and that cite a hunk the revision no longer holds reopen it. A record no round names reopens nothing. A dismissal made again after that round, or at the revision read, holds. A chain that names a round the set lacks, a round whose record the set lacks, or a round of another changeset leaves the finding live, and so does a chain, or one branch of a fork, that ends before the dismissal's revision; a fork where either branch cites a changed hunk reopens it, and one where neither does keeps it. `review-records-export.test.ts` has three hosts review three revisions, the second and third with no history of the changeset: each round names the one before it, and the dismissal the first host made still reaches the third round's reader. With a forked store, a fresh host's round names both branches, and when host A reviews again after host B, A's round names both its own earlier round and B's.
