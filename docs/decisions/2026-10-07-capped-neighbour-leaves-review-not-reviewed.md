# A neighbour at its findings budget leaves the review not reviewed

Choice: A lens that reaches its findings budget and is refused a further report records that, with the budget. When another running lens could have handed it defects through a hand-off that rendered, the review's record for that lens is `ended`, with a reason naming the budget and the lenses that could have handed it defects, and the verdict reads not reviewed. A lens that reported exactly its budget, and was never refused, ran. A lens nothing handed a defect to ran, as did one whose hand-off was left out for size: the lens that would have handed it kept those defects. `budget.ended: count` does not count a findings budget's end as run; it counts a tokens or tools budget's end, which the neighbour's author has accepted, and says nothing of defects another lens gave up.

Why: Problem: the handing lens leaves a defect to its neighbour, and the neighbour's findings budget refuses the report. Neither lens reports it, and the verdict read reviewed. Example: `correctness` leaves a broken caller to `contracts`; `contracts` reports its budget of findings and is refused the next, so a defect lost sits in no report. The handing lens cannot know, since the lenses run together. Solution: the other choice, holding the hand-off only for a neighbour that did not end at its cap, needs the neighbour's answer before the handing lens runs, or a second run of the handing lens, which costs tokens for every capped neighbour. Recording the refusal costs nothing and states the loss. The review is not reviewed rather than silently short, and the maintainer can raise the budget or run the lens again. A refusal is the evidence, not the count: a lens that reports its budget's worth and stops left nothing unreported that anyone can show. `handedBy` records that a hand-off instruction rendered for the neighbour, not that the handing lens found a defect, so the not-reviewed result is conservative: a rerun costs less than a defect lost.
