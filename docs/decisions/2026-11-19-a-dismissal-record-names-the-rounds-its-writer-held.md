# A dismissal record names the rounds its writer held

Amends: [the decision that a dismissal record is written once](2026-11-18-a-dismissal-record-is-written-once.md), "A dismissal made with no store gets its record at the next review that exports"
Amends: [the decision that a shared reader remembers the hunks cited since a dismissal](2026-11-18-a-shared-reader-remembers-every-cited-hunk.md), which rounds count as since a dismissal, in place of the round chain, and the rule for a cut chain
Amends: [the shared record decision](2026-11-13-shared-record-behind-a-store-port.md), rule 7's "the record reaches the store with the host's next add", for a dismissal made with no store

Status: for [issue #481](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/481), on [pull request #575](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/pull/575). The coordinator answered on 2026-11-19: a dismissal made with no store exports no record, and the record names a frontier. That is a coordinator's answer, not a ruling. On 2026-11-20 the coordinator chose that the reader tests every round of the set outside the frontier, not only the read round's chain, as rule 7 of [the shared record decision](2026-11-13-shared-record-behind-a-store-port.md) already reads; that too is a coordinator's answer, not a ruling. It meets the maintainer's ruling of 2026-11-18 on a cut chain by testing what such a chain would have hidden, as the section on it says.

## Problem

The reader took the rounds since a dismissal from the round chain, and stopped at the rounds of the revision the dismissal's sighting names. A revision is not a time. A round reviewed again at that revision after the dismissal is a round since it, and its sightings can cite a hunk the revision lacks.

Example: a finding is dismissed at revision 1 after round R1. Round R1' then reviews revision 1 again, and its sighting cites hunk B, which revision 1 no longer holds. The host reopens the finding. The reader stopped at R1', since it is of the dismissal's revision, and kept the dismissal.

## Decision

`melian dismiss --records` names, in the record's `frontier`, every round of the changeset that the store's one listing holds as the dismissal is made, in ID order. The reader counts as since the dismissal every round of the changeset in the set that is neither in the frontier nor an ancestor of a round there, whatever its revision and wherever it sits, the round it reads among them. It tests them in no order. `before` now serves only to find the frontier's ancestors: the rounds the writer held without listing them, because a round its store held already named them.

The field is optional in the schema. A record without it, which an earlier build wrote, counts as no dismissal: its finding stays live. That is the strict direction. No shared store holds such a record yet, so nothing is lost.

Only the writer, as it dismisses, knows what its store held. So a dismissal made with no store gets no record. A review names it as unshared, under the code `madeWithoutStore`, and `melian dismiss` says so. To share it, dismiss it again with `--records`. The verification request's key still names such a dismissal, by the ID its record would have with no frontier and no `supersedes`, so a replaced dismissal still asks the verifier again.

A record found in the store for a dismissal made again is the one the store holds, whatever its frontier, as a record is already found whatever its `supersedes`.

## Every round outside the frontier

A reader that follows only the chain back from the round it reads misses a round another host wrote without the frontier. Example: a finding is dismissed after round R1, so the frontier is [R1]. Host B, which never saw R1, writes R2 with `before: []`, and its sighting cites a hunk the dismissed revision lacks. Host A then writes R3, which names R1 alone. A reader of R3 that walks R3's chain reaches only R1, which the frontier holds, and keeps the dismissal; R2 is in the set and would have reopened it. Rule 7 reads the set, so R2 reopens it whatever R3 names.

So the reader takes every round of the changeset in the set and leaves out only the frontier and its ancestors. A tested round that names a record the set does not hold, or follows a round the set does not hold that the writer did not hold either, leaves the finding live, since what that round cited is unknown. So does a read round of that kind. A revision the reader cannot resolve still refuses the round, as below.

The reader may now block where the host that wrote the round read passes. That host holds only the rounds it ran and the ones it listed, and so can hold a finding dismissed while a round it never saw reopened it. The reader's answer is the set's, by rule 7, and the set's is the one that counts. The writer dismisses again once it has seen that round, if the dismissal still holds.

## A cut chain

The maintainer's ruling of 2026-11-18, given in the coordinator's session, is recorded in [the shared-reader decision](2026-11-18-a-shared-reader-remembers-every-cited-hunk.md) in these words: when "any branch of the chain ends, at a round with an empty `before`, before it reaches a round of the dismissal's revision", "the reader counts such a dismissal as nothing". The ruling kept a reader from trusting a chain whose cut part may have cited a hunk that has since changed.

The reader no longer walks a chain to find the rounds since a dismissal, so no chain can be cut. Every round the set holds outside the frontier is tested, the ones past any cut included. A round the set does not hold still leaves the finding live, as above. The ruling is met by the wider test, and the restatement of it for the frontier that this decision held until 2026-11-20 is removed.

## Each round at its own revision

The host tests each sighting against the hunks of the revision it was seen at. A reader that tests every sighting against the revision it reads misses a reopening. Example: the dismissal accepts hunks in `src/a.ts` and `src/b.ts` at revision X. Revision Y reverts `src/a.ts`, and Y's round sights the finding citing `src/b.ts` alone, so the host reopens it there. Revision Z changes both files again. Tested only at Z, the reader would find both hunks and let the dismissal hold.

So the reader resolves the revision of each round since the dismissal from git, by its base and head, with `Changeset.resolve`, the diff the CLI reads the read round's own revision from. It tests that round's sightings of the finding against the hunks the dismissal accepted and every hunk cited in any round since, at that revision's hunks, with `sharedDismissalHolds`, the rule it uses for the read round. A sighting that reopens there makes the dismissal count as nothing. A round with no sighting of the finding needs no revision, so its commits are never read. A round after a retarget has another base, and it is tested at its own base and head, never at the read round's. The coordinator chose this on 2026-11-20, after the maintainer said he had no preference among the options; it is the coordinator's choice, not a ruling.

A reader that cannot resolve such a revision refuses the round, as not reviewed, and names why: a commit the checkout lacks, by its hash, as a shallow clone may, or a read with no checkout at all. It never lets the dismissal hold. The refusal says how to recover: fetch the history, or dismiss the finding again with `--records`. A new record's frontier names every round its writer's store held, the lost one included, so the reader never asks for that round's revision again; a pull request whose force push dropped a round's commits is recovered the same way, as the coordinator asked on 2026-11-20. So `melian findings --records`, and the gate [issue #395](https://github.com/melian-agent/melian/issues/395) builds on this reader, need the repository's history of every round they read past a dismissal.

## Tests

`recorded-round.test.ts` reopens a dismissal once a round after it, at its own revision, cites a hunk the revision lacks. It keeps a dismissal whose writer held the rounds that cited such hunks. It drops one whose record names no frontier. It reopens a dismissal on a sibling round another host wrote without the frontier, which the round read never names, and keeps one past a round that follows none, or a fork with such a branch, while neither cites anything new. `review-records.test.ts` checks that `made` names only the changeset's rounds, sorted, and finds a held record whatever its frontier. `review-payloads.test.ts` accepts a dismissal with no frontier and refuses one listed out of order or twice. `review-records-export.test.ts` shares no dismissal made with no store, and names it as unshared. `recorded-round.test.ts` also reopens a dismissal at Z once Y, in the example above, lacked a hunk it accepted. It tests a round after a retarget at its own base, which reopens the dismissal when that base took a file the dismissal accepted a hunk in, and keeps it when the base changed only another file. It refuses a round whose chain names a commit the checkout lacks, naming the commit, and one read with no checkout, unless no round since sights the finding. A property test builds 24 seeded histories of one to three rounds, each on the base or a retargeted one, with random contents and random cited hunks. It drives the host's own lifecycle, `upsertFinding` and `dismissFinding`, over the same sightings at the same revisions read from git, and checks that the reader never keeps a dismissal the host reopened.

`review-records-export.test.ts` runs a second property test over two or three hosts that share one store and one repository, each holding only the rounds it ran, against an oracle host that ran them all. It checks that the reader never keeps a dismissal the oracle reopened, over 20 seeds by default and any number `MELIAN_PROPERTY_SEEDS` names, and keeps seeds 9 and 302 as named cases. A third gives each host a store of its own and copies one host's store into another's at random steps, so a host can write a round without having seen another's. Its oracle is rule 7 over every store's records: a dismissal no other supersedes holds when every round its writer did not hold reviewed the line beside the finding as the dismissed revision had it. The reader must agree with it on every seed, and seed 112 stays as a named case: the reader that walked the chain kept the dismissal there, the set reopens it, and the host that wrote the round read still holds it dismissed. `recorded-round.test.ts` tests no round its writer held before its frontier, whether the frontier names it or a round two before it, and keeps a dismissal whose frontier names a round this set lacks. A frontier that names only such a round, before a round that follows none, keeps the dismissal while that round cites nothing new.
